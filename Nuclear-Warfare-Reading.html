New York Times

Nuclear War: The Rising Risk

500 word reflection

This article capitalizes on the motion of scroll-reading that is utilized for storytelling in fiction (e.g. Webtoon) but not often utilized in nonfiction! I found the opening animation really powerful because it was immediately eye-catching. The red blaring alarm sets a tense atmosphere centered around the risk of nuclear-warfare. Due to the red alarm being commonly associated with public safety emergencies, the interactive scroll environment immediately placed me in a state of fight-or-flight.
I appreciated the limited use of the multimedia animation throughout the piece. I feel that each of the transitions were placed well to smoothen the transition from traditional news article to scroll-storytelling. While some transitions functioned to immerse the reader into the horrific reality of potential nuclear destruction, others functioned to display data in a more emotionally prevalent way.
The animated explosion sequence is the perfect example of multimedia storytelling made to immerse the viewer. The unique affordance of this medium is that the viewer can fully control the pace. As a result, the animations have a quality of distorted time in which the viewer can choose to reverse the image and linger on certain details. However, the number of frames given to a certain animation also influences the “frame rate” along with the speed that we naturally read certain texts. I found it very impressive that NYT managed to retain the effect of Slow in and Slow out despite mapping the explosive animation as a slow-motion controlled by the audience.
My favorite interactive bit is the timelapsed map of each country’s growing nuclear arsenal. While it is not uncommon nowadays to view a map that displays data, it is uncommon to combine that idea with a scrollable timelapse. The immersion is not broken but rather fueled by the fact that we continue to scroll past sections of the article, as if the article is a video that continues narrating over the graphed data.
Another small design choice that I loved is the way that immersive text is unveiled when placed on the black, bleak background. Despite the news article scrolling up, the white text remains stuck on the page until the entire page is covered in black. The bold way in which NYT introduces the more interactive segments is an effective signal to the reader that reads similarly to a theater dimming house lights before a performance. The choice to have slow fade-ins to the immersion rather than an abrupt cut (i.e. the fade-in of toxic rain) is powerful to force the reader to slow down and truly take in the atmosphere.
Overall, I feel that the user experience is greatly enhanced by the minimalist aesthetic. With a lot of multimedia interactive aspects complicating our experience of the story, the text needed to be as minimalistic as possible. For instance, the integration of video footage in the article feels less like a distraction / re-iteration and more like another means of immersion. This might be a direct example of the Aesthetic Usability Effect which is missing from a lot of other news articles piled with pop-up video ads. I argue that the NYT leverages their subscription model to the fullest by breaking free of the limiting conventions of an ad-filled site.
